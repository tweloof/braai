Monkey gland sauce

The great South African steakhouse sauce — sweet, tangy, faintly mysterious, and containing neither monkeys nor glands. A braai.co.za page since 2010; still the best thing that can happen to a steak that's already had the best thing happen to it.

Fire's edge or stovetop Makes ±500 ml 25 minutes

The great South African steakhouse sauce — sweet, tangy, faintly mysterious, and containing neither monkeys nor glands. A braai.co.za page since 2010; still the best thing that can happen to a steak that's already had the best thing happen to it.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp butter or oil
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 250 ml tomato sauce
  • 125 ml fruit chutney
  • 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp brown vinegar
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tsp mustard powder, pinch of chilli

Method

  1. Soften the onion in butter at the edge of the fire; add the garlic for the last minute.
  2. Stir in everything else with a splash of water.
  3. Simmer lazily for 15–20 minutes until glossy and spoon-coating, stirring now and then.
  4. Spoon over steak, burgers or wors. Refuses to explain its name; store a week in the fridge.

Questions the fire actually gets

Does monkey gland sauce contain monkey?

No. No monkeys and no glands. It is onion, garlic, tomato sauce, fruit chutney, Worcestershire sauce, soy sauce, brown vinegar, brown sugar, mustard powder and a pinch of chilli.

How long does monkey gland sauce take?

About 25 minutes. Soften the onion in butter, garlic for the last minute, then simmer everything with a splash of water for 15–20 minutes until glossy and spoon-coating.

What do you put it on?

Steak, burgers or wors. It keeps a week in the fridge. For the steak itself, buy thick — rump, sirloin, ribeye or T-bone — which is the beef note on The Cuts.
