Why Braai Day?

Heritage Day, 24 September, is the public holiday on which South Africans celebrate who we are — all of who we are. In 2026 it falls on a Thursday. The National Braai Day idea grew from a simple observation: whatever our differences, the fire is where all of us already gather. It's the most South African form of unity there is — nobody has to agree on anything except that the coals are almost ready.

So the brief is simple: on 24 September, light a fire, gather your people, and feed them. This page is your planning kit. Nothing here is for sale.

On this page: what to buy, which wood, the timeline, the menu, the mistakes, if it rains, and the questions.

What to buy, and how much

Work from the people, not from a fantasy menu. The only quantity this site will put a number on is the meat: 400–500 g per adult. Eight adults is about 3.2–4 kg. Mix it so the hungry ones have something early and the show-offs have tjops. A bring-and-braai means guests carry some of that meat themselves — the host still brings the fire.

Wood, before you need it

Dense hardwood burns down to long coals. Kameeldoring (camel thorn) is the gold standard and a protected tree — buy it from a permitted supplier. Sekelbos is the bushveld favourite. Rooikrans is the Cape workhorse, and an invasive alien, so burning it is a small act of conservation. Good hardwood needs 45–60 minutes to become coals. If flames are still licking the grid, you have a fire, not yet a braai. The hand test, palm at grid height: 4 seconds hot, 6 hot-medium, 8 medium, 10 gentle. Briquettes are a fair backup if the bags are gone; gas is a Tuesday-night convenience, not the Heritage Day fire. The full comparison is the Wood & Coals guide.

The Braai Day timeline

These times assume you want to eat around 15:00. Light the hardwood at 13:00 so it has that 45–60 minutes. The braai starts when the fire is lit, not when the meat goes on.

The menu, sorted

Same meat budget as above — 400–500 g per adult — and let the classics carry it. These are pages that already exist. Nothing new has been invented for the occasion.

  • Boerewors rolls with tomato smoor — the anchor.
  • Chicken sosaties — on early, so nobody waits hungry.
  • Lamb tjops, or a butterflied leg when you are feeding a crowd.
  • Pap en sous — this is how the meat stretches.
  • Braaibroodjies over dying coals, butter on the outside. Not a side. The exam.
  • Roosterkoek, if the dough went out at 11:30.
  • Lamb ribbetjies or pork spare ribs when someone has the patience.
  • On the coast: snoek, butterflied, flat, skin-side down.
  • Potato bake for the table, and chocolate bananas when the coals are dying. Or a peppermint tart from the fridge. This is not the day for plated desserts.

The whole shelf is fifteen recipes. A potjie if someone is staying the afternoon and can be trusted not to stir.

Mistakes that empty a grid

  1. Meat on flames. Flames are for show. Coals make the braai. If fire is still licking the grid, you are early.
  2. Stabbing the wors. The juice you lose is the flavour you promised everyone. Tongs, not a fork.
  3. Braaibroodjies on a hot fire. They want low coals, and butter on the outside — always on the outside.
  4. Wood bought on Thursday morning. When Braai Day is a Thursday, Wednesday is the wood day.
  5. Counting the tjops. Buy the upper end of 400–500 g per adult, then stop doing maths at the grid.
  6. Pressing the steak. That squeezes out the part you paid for.
  7. Cancelling for weather. The fire does not move indoors. The guests do. See below.

If the weather misbehaves

Weather is a rumour. Rain moves the guests, not the fire. Garage door up, the stoep, or a big umbrella. Keep the grid out of the wet if you can. Mind the wind before you light, and keep the fire away from thatch and overhanging branches. Ash stays in the braai until it is stone cold. The fire guide has the safety notes in full.

Questions people actually ask

What is National Braai Day?

National Braai Day is the day South Africans light a fire and gather around it, on purpose, as a country. It falls on Heritage Day. The idea is simple: whatever else we disagree about, the fire is where we already meet. This page is the planning kit — quantities, wood, a timeline and the menu.

Is National Braai Day the same as Heritage Day?

Same date, two names. Heritage Day is the South African public holiday on 24 September. National Braai Day is how a great many South Africans choose to spend it: one fire, the people you like, and enough food. The public holiday is Heritage Day. The braai is the celebration.

When is National Braai Day 2026?

Thursday 24 September 2026. The countdown on this page runs until the start of that day, and through the day itself. Next year it falls on 24 September again — the date does not move.

How much meat per person for a braai?

Plan 400–500 g of meat per adult. Eight adults is about 3.2–4 kg, mixed so nobody stands hungry: wors, something quick like sosaties, and tjops or chops. Pap, rolls and braaibroodjies stretch it. A bring-and-braai means guests carry some of the meat themselves.

What wood should I use on Braai Day?

Dense hardwood that burns down to long coals: kameeldoring (camel thorn) from a permitted supplier — it is a protected tree — sekelbos inland, and rooikrans in the Cape, where burning that invasive alien is a small bit of conservation. Good hardwood needs 45–60 minutes before it is coals. Briquettes are a fair backup if the wood is gone. Buy it the day before. On the morning of the 24th, everyone else already has.

How do I plan the Braai Day timeline?

Work backwards from eating around 15:00. Collect the meat in the morning. Light the fire at 13:00 so hardwood can become coals. Pap goes on at the edge around 13:45. When the hand test says go — about 14:00 — sosaties and tjops, then the wors. Rest the meat under foil while braaibroodjies take the dying coals. The braai starts when the fire is lit, not when the meat goes on.

What if it rains on Braai Day?

Weather is a rumour. Rain moves the guests, not the fire. Garage door up, the stoep, or a big umbrella. Keep the grid out of the wet if you can. The coals do not take a vote.

The only to-do list that matters: wood (twice as much as you think), meat from a proper butcher, ice (three times as much as you think), matches, and people you like. Everything else is decoration.
