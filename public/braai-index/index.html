The basket, on the table

Three kilograms of meat that a South African Saturday actually understands: wors for the rolls, pork loin chops because somebody always asks for them, and a chicken braai pack so the cousins who arrived unannounced still eat. One kilo of each. No lamb — that is a different argument, and a different number.

Wood is deliberately left out. A bag of permitted braai wood is a local fact, not a national one, and this page will not pretend Pretoria and Plettenberg Bay pay the same for kameeldoring.

Where the numbers came from

These are latest-known example figures from public South African grocery catalogues and shelf listings in the week of 24–30 August 2026. Mid-range prices — not the deepest midweek special, not a butcher’s quote, not a live scrape. Boerewors is the legally ordinary kind: at least 90% meat. If your butcher is cheaper, lekker. If yours is dearer, you already knew.

  • Boerewors, R110/kg. Public supermarket listings for typical thick beef-and-pork wors sat around R110; own-brand farmstyle was nearer R90 on special and about R120 off it.
  • Pork loin chops, R90/kg. Catalogue chops that week ran from about R65/kg on a deep bulk special to around R120/kg regular. We took the middle, not the flyer’s loudest sticker.
  • Chicken braai pack, R80/kg. A typical 5 kg frozen pack listed at R399 — R79.80 a kilo, rounded.
How this page stays honest: the number lives in /data/braai-index.json. Next week’s updater changes that file. The HTML does not get rewritten to invent a bargain. No affiliate links. No “buy here”. No brand placements. The site sells nothing.
