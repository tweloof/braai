Roosterkoek

Bread, but braaied — crisp outside, steamy inside, torn open and buttered while too hot to hold. Shop rolls are for emergencies.

Gentle coals · 10-second hand test Makes 8 2 hours (incl. proving)

Bread, but braaied — crisp outside, steamy inside, torn open and buttered while too hot to hold. Shop rolls are for emergencies.

Ingredients

  • 500 g white bread flour
  • 1 sachet (10 g) instant yeast
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • ±300 ml lukewarm water
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • Butter, jam or cheese, to serve

Method

  1. Mix the dry ingredients, add the water and oil, and knead for a good 10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
  2. Cover and prove somewhere warm until doubled — about an hour.
  3. Knock down gently, divide into 8, shape into rough squares and rest another 15 minutes.
  4. Braai on the cooler side of the grid over gentle coals, turning every few minutes, for 15–20 minutes. They're done when they sound hollow when tapped.
  5. Break open, butter, and accept the compliments with humility you don't feel.

Which fire, and which day? Gentle coals, the 10-second hand test, on the cooler side of the grid. Hold your palm at grid height and count: 4 seconds is hot, 6 is hot-medium, 8 is medium, 10 or more is gentle. The full reading is the hand test, and the wood that makes those coals is in the wood guide. Buying notes: the meat they are eaten with. Wednesday 24 September is National Braai Day. Questions the fire actually gets

What heat for roosterkoek?

Gentle coals, the 10-second hand test, on the cooler side of the grid. Turn every few minutes for 15–20 minutes. They are done when they sound hollow when tapped.

How long does roosterkoek take?

About two hours including proving. Knead 10 minutes, prove about an hour until doubled, rest 15 minutes after shaping, then braai. Start the dough when you light the fire, not when the coals are ready.

What do you eat roosterkoek with?

Butter, jam or cheese, torn open while too hot to hold. On a Braai Day fire they share the gentle side with the braaibroodjies, after the wors.
