Braaibroodjies

The true test of a braaier is not the steak — it's whether the braaibroodjies come off golden, melted and unburnt while everyone is shouting for the meat. Made over the dying coals, after the meat, with patience.

Low coals · 10-second hand test Makes 6 25 minutes

The true test of a braaier is not the steak — it's whether the braaibroodjies come off golden, melted and unburnt while everyone is shouting for the meat. Made over the dying coals, after the meat, with patience.

Ingredients

  • 12 slices white bread
  • Soft butter
  • Chutney
  • 200 g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 tomatoes, thinly sliced
  • 1 onion, very thinly sliced
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Butter the outside of every slice — the side that touches the grid. This is the whole secret.
  2. Inside: chutney on one slice, then cheese, tomato, onion. Season the tomato layer. Close.
  3. Pack them snugly into a hinged grid (toeklap-rooster) so they can be flipped as one.
  4. Toast over low coals, turning every minute or two, for 8–12 minutes. When they're deep golden and the cheese surrenders, they're done. Cut diagonally. Obviously.

Which fire, and which day? Low coals, the 10-second hand test, after the meat. Hold your palm at grid height and count: 4 seconds is hot, 6 is hot-medium, 8 is medium, 10 or more is gentle. Questions the fire actually gets

Which side of a braaibroodjie gets the butter?

The outside — the side that touches the grid. That is the whole secret. Chutney, cheese, tomato and onion go on the inside, and the tomato gets the salt and pepper.

What heat for braaibroodjies?

Low coals, the 10-second hand test, once the meat is resting. Toast them in a hinged toeklap-rooster for 8–12 minutes, turning every minute or two, until deep golden and the cheese has melted.

When do braaibroodjies go on for Braai Day?

Last, on the gentle side of a two-zone fire, while the wors and sosaties rest. They burn on a hot grid and sulk on a cold one.
