South African braai recipes

Sixteen South African braai recipes, each on its own page — the story, the method, and the engineer’s table, where ingredients merge left-to-right into supper. Written the way the ooms and tannies actually make them. What the latest published family basket costs is the National Braai Index.

24 September · every year

A Braai Day menu for a crowd

Five classics, in the order the fire actually wants them. Each page has the method and says how many it feeds — multiply for the cousins.

  1. The day before. Chicken sosaties into the apricot-curry marinade, overnight. Thighs, not breast. Buying note: chicken on The Cuts.
  2. When you light. Mix the roosterkoek dough. It wants about an hour to prove while the wood becomes coals. Hardwood from a permitted supplier, 45–60 minutes: which wood.
  3. While the flames die. Stywe pap en tamatiesous at the edge of the fire, and the tomato smoor from the boerewors rolls.
  4. At the 8-second hand test. The wors coil, whole, never pierced, and the sosaties. How to count. Boerewors is at least 90% meat.
  5. At 10 seconds, while the meat rests. Braaibroodjies and the roosterkoek on the gentle side. The day itself: National Braai Day.

If your family’s version is better, send the recipe under your real name — or put one sentence on The Braai so the communal fire stays fed.

25 December · 26 December · 31 December

Festive season braai: Christmas, Day of Goodwill and New Year

The dates do not move, and neither does the fire. A Christmas braai on 25 December, the same idea on 26 December (Day of Goodwill), and a New Year’s braai from 31 December into 1 January. No year attached. The menu below uses only recipes already on this site.

A suggested festive menu. Pick a centrepiece, something for whoever arrives early, and the sides the grid can hold. You do not have to cook all of it.

  • Butterflied leg of lamb, 2–2.5 kg, feeds eight. On the coast, butterflied snoek (1.5–2 kg, skin-side down, serves six) can take that place instead.
  • Sticky pork spare ribs, 1.5 kg, serves four. The first cook is done before the day.
  • Boerewors rolls with tomato smoor — 1 kg of wors is six rolls — and monkey gland sauce (500 ml) if the wors or a steak wants it.
  • Stywe pap en tamatiesous and the braai potato bake, each written for eight. They stretch the meat.
  • Roosterkoek (eight) and braaibroodjies (six) once the coals drop. Chocolate bananas (six) on the dying coals.

Firing order

  1. The day before. Lamb into the yoghurt, lemon, garlic and rosemary — at least four hours, ideally overnight. Ribs in foil with a splash of water, about 1½ hours at 160 °C (oven, or a kettle braai with indirect heat), tender but not falling apart. Buy hardwood from a permitted supplier. A long day eats coals: wood and the wait for them.
  2. When you light. Mix the roosterkoek dough. It wants about an hour to prove. Good hardwood needs 45–60 minutes to become coals. Flames on the grid means it is still a fire, not yet a braai.
  3. While the flames die. Potato bake sealed in foil — kettle braai, or beside medium coals with coals on the lid — 60–75 minutes, then 15 under cheddar. Pap at the edge, steamed 30–40 minutes, and the tomato smoor (onions about 10 minutes, then 25–30 until jammy). Monkey gland sauce, if you are making it, is about 25 minutes at that same edge.
  4. At the 8-second hand test. Lamb flat, fat side down first, 35–45 minutes, turning every 10 minutes or so, then a 10-minute rest under foil. Ribs for their last 10–15 minutes on that medium fire; the sugar in the baste burns on anything hotter. Wors coil whole, never pierced, 12–15 minutes. Snoek, if that is the fish and not the lamb: skin-side down for nearly the whole 20–25 minutes, flesh flipped only at the end. One centrepiece owns the grid. The Cuts is the buying note for whatever else you add.
  5. At 10 seconds, while the meat rests. Braaibroodjies and roosterkoek on the gentle side. Chocolate bananas, slit, chocolate in, foil on, 8–10 minutes, when the plates are already dirty.

Quantities and the clock

Plan 400–500 g of meat per adult. Eight adults is about 3.2–4 kg. That is the only meat figure this site publishes; the National Braai Day page is the method, and it works in December too. Pap, rolls and braaibroodjies stretch it. Work backwards from when you want to eat. Light the hardwood 45–60 minutes before the hand test says go. Start the potato bake and the pap while the flames are still dying. The leg wants 35–45 minutes plus the rest.

Heat, shade and a dry December

Shade for the people, and shade for the raw meat. Cold drinks stay cold. Meat comes out of the fridge for the 30–45 minutes the fire already asks — in this heat, not an hour in the sun — and cooked food does not sit out while everyone swims. Keep a bucket of water or sand within reach, for the fire, not for pouring on the coals. Ash stays in the braai until it is stone cold. In a dry area, check your municipality and the fire-danger index before you light. If there is a fire ban, the open wood fire stays out. Use gas, or a closed braai, only where those are allowed.

Questions the fire actually gets

What should you cook for a Braai Day crowd?

Five classics, in the order the fire wants them. The day before: chicken sosaties in the apricot-curry marinade, overnight, thigh meat not breast. When you light: roosterkoek dough, which needs about an hour to prove while the wood becomes coals. While the flames die: stywe pap en tamatiesous at the edge, and the tomato smoor for the boerewors rolls. At the 8-second hand test: the wors coil, whole and never pierced, and the sosaties. At 10 seconds, while the meat rests: braaibroodjies and the roosterkoek on the gentle side.

What is the braai hand test?

Hold your palm at grid height and count. Four seconds is hot, six is hot-medium, eight is medium, ten or more is gentle. Wors and sosaties want the eight-second fire. Braaibroodjies and roosterkoek want ten or more. The full reading, and which wood makes those coals, is on the Wood & Coals guide.

How much food does the Braai Day menu make?

Each recipe states its own yield: six boerewors rolls, twelve sosatie skewers, pap for eight, six braaibroodjies, eight roosterkoek. Multiply for a bigger crowd. This is a firing order.

What do South Africans braai for Christmas?

There is no single Christmas braai, and this site will not invent one. A practical festive menu, from recipes already on these pages: a butterflied leg of lamb as the centrepiece, or butterflied snoek, skin-side down, if the coast sent fish; sticky pork spare ribs finished on the coals; boerewors rolls with tomato smoor for whoever arrives early; stywe pap en tamatiesous and a potato bake to stretch the meat; roosterkoek and braaibroodjies on the gentle side; monkey gland sauce if the wors or a steak wants it; chocolate bananas on the dying coals. The same fire on 25 December, on 26 December (Day of Goodwill), and from 31 December into 1 January.

How much meat per person for a Christmas braai?

Plan 400–500 g of meat per adult, the same figure this site uses for any crowd. Eight adults is about 3.2–4 kg. Each festive recipe states its own yield: the butterflied leg feeds eight, snoek six, spare ribs four, one kilogram of wors makes six rolls, and pap and the potato bake each feed eight. Multiply for a bigger crowd. Pap, rolls and braaibroodjies stretch the meat. The method is on National Braai Day, and what belongs on the grid is on The Cuts.

Can I braai on Christmas Day if there is a fire ban?

Check your local municipality and the fire-danger index before you light. An open wood fire in a dry area is a veld-fire risk, and a ban means that wood fire stays out. Use gas, or a closed braai, only where those are allowed. Ash from any fire stays in the braai until it is stone cold.

When do you light the fire for a New Year's braai?

Work backwards from when you want to eat. Hardwood from a permitted supplier needs 45–60 minutes to become coals, so buy it the day before. The day before the meal, the lamb goes into its marinade for at least four hours, ideally overnight, and the spare ribs take their first cook, about 1½ hours at 160 °C. When you light, mix the roosterkoek dough. While the flames die, start the potato bake, the pap, the tomato smoor and the monkey gland sauce if you are making it. At the 8-second hand test the lamb needs 35–45 minutes, snoek 20–25 minutes skin-side down, the wors 12–15 minutes whole and never pierced, and the ribs their last 10–15 minutes. At 10 seconds, while the meat rests, braaibroodjies and roosterkoek, then chocolate bananas on the dying coals. The same order serves 25 December, 26 December and 1 January.

A note on the hand test: hold your palm at grid height above the coals and count. If you last 4 seconds it's hot, 6 is hot-medium, 8 is medium, 10 or more is gentle.
